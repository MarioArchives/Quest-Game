Mystery Pub Crawl · design decision · the countdown

What do players see while they wait for the case to open?

Today the game sits in the lobby until the GM taps Open case, and the Missions folder only says “Missions open once the GM starts the game.” Now the GM sets a start time and can move it; players count down to it after the booking desk, and at zero the case opens on its own. Five ideas for the player’s side, from the days before to the second it opens, including how a player finds out that the GM moved the time.

A

The departures board

Until the case opens, the app is a station departures board: one service, the 19:00 Last Orders, with its status and the time to departure in split flaps.

Full-screen takeover · tabs hidden until the start

The idea

One train, one platform: the board shows when, from where (sealed until the GM reveals stop 01) and how long to go. A late start reads the way a delayed train does: DELAYED, EXPECTED 19:30, and an announcement saying who changed it.

What’s good

Everybody already knows how to read a delay on a departures board. Zero is a real moment, with the whole board spinning at once, and the flaps make every change visible as it happens.

Cost / risk

A takeover hides the tabs for days, so My file and the Log book need the two buttons. It is a new full-screen look outside the case-file kit, and the flap animation needs building (and a still version for reduced motion).

B

The sealed case file

Recommended: it sits exactly where the missions will appear, so zero hands over in place (the seal breaks and the stack is underneath), and the points chip, which has nothing to say before the start, carries the countdown to every other tab.

Front folder of the Missions stack + the points chip
Recommended

The idea

The Missions tab’s empty folder becomes the case file itself, tied with twine and sealed in red wax with the bee: “Not to be opened before 19:00 Sat 7 Nov 2026”. The countdown sits under the seal; the app bar’s points chip shows it on every other tab.

What’s good

It uses the kit players already know (manila, twine, kraft tags, the sealed chapters’ wax) and replaces the one line that says “Missions open once the GM starts the game”. A move is written on the file itself: the old time struck in red pen, re-stamped, plus a toast and a case update.

Cost / risk

Less theatre than a takeover: a player on another tab only sees the chip change. The broken seal needs a short animation, and the chip goes back to points at zero, so its meaning changes under the player’s thumb.

C

Chalk on the wall

Somebody has chalked the countdown on the interrogation room’s wall, right above the Missions folder: tally marks for the days, the time circled, the meeting point when the GM gives it.

On the wall above the Missions folder · Missions tab only

The idea

The charcoal wall the whole app sits on becomes a blackboard. Days are tally marks, the night is a big hand-written number, and a change is chalk wiped and rewritten, so the smear of the old time stays visible next to the new one.

What’s good

Warm and human, and it costs no new paper: the folder stays exactly as built, just pushed down. The smear tells a player what changed even if they missed the push.

Cost / risk

Lots of hand-lettering to lay out (Caveat with a chalk filter), and live seconds look odd in chalk, so it would tick in minutes until the last ten. Only the Missions tab shows it, and it pushes the folder down by 300 px for days.

D

The teleprinter

A paper tape ticks along under the app bar on every tab, and the Missions folder holds the full telex. Every change prints as a new numbered message, in red ribbon when it’s a flash.

A tape on every tab + the telex in the Missions folder

The idea

Police signals, the old way: messages headed ZCZC and ended NNNN, numbered and time-stamped (291448Z OCT), from the GM to all suspects. The tape is the one-line version, ticking on every tab.

What’s good

It shows on every tab without taking any over, and the message history answers “what changed, and when”: MMP001 said 1900, MMP002 says 1930. The stop 01 reveal arrives the same way.

Cost / risk

A 30 px strip on every screen for days, and capitals in mono are slow to read. The tape has to stop moving for reduced motion. The telex voice is a new tone next to the case files.

E

The calendar chip

The countdown lives only in the app bar, where the points chip has nothing to say before the start: a desk-calendar page counting days, a stopwatch on the night. Tap it for the calendar.

The points chip on every tab · nothing else changes

The idea

Before the case opens everybody has 0 points, so the chip can do another job. Days away it is a tear-off calendar page (9 DAYS); on the night a stopwatch whose ring drains. Its pop-over is a wall calendar of the fortnight with the days crossed off.

What’s good

Everywhere at once, the smallest build, and “Add to my calendar” puts the night in their phone, with “Update my calendar” after a move. The Missions folder keeps its empty state with the time written in.

Cost / risk

Small: a move is easy to miss without the push, and zero is a chip changing rather than a moment. The calendar page and the points chip share a spot, which may confuse at 19:30.

My pick: B

B, the sealed case file. The countdown belongs where the missions will be, and B puts it there: the Missions tab’s front folder is the case file, sealed until 19:00, and at zero the seal breaks and the stack is underneath, so the hand-over needs no extra screen. The points chip, idle until the first points, carries the count to the other tabs. A move is written on the file in red pen and re-stamped, with a toast in the app and a case update for anyone away from it.

Worth borrowing: A’s DELAYED / EXPECTED wording for the push and the toast; D’s numbered messages as Log book entries (“Start moved to 19:30 by the GM, 16:02”), so the history is kept; E’s “Add to my calendar” on the sealed file. C’s smear of the old time is the same idea as B’s struck-through time, in chalk.
