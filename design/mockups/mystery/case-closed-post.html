Case closed: Natalia’s desk, round two

What changed from round one

  1. The paperA broadsheet like your reference: a serif masthead between rules, a condensed headline, the photo with a piece beside it, columns underneath.
  2. RedactedAll but a sentence or two is blacked out in lines, so each page looks full and the eye goes to what matters.
  3. Bigger typeBody text up from 10.5 to 13 px, headlines from 22 to 34 px; the papers are wider too.
  4. Six papersDown from ten: Bob dead, six recruited, the weapon, the time, the scene, the verdict.
  5. New orderYour crawl, then your graphs, then your profile, which lands last and unfolds into your achievements.
  6. GraphsThe points chart is twice the size, with the table’s race under it on the same page.

The deskNatalia’s · chosen

A dark room and a desk. Six folded papers fall one milestone at a time; your crawl and your graphs land open on top; your profile lands last and unfolds into your achievements

Moves · wait or tap: each paper lands on the pile. The last one unfolds to fill the screen; scroll it. Look · lamp light on wood, a broadsheet with redacted columns, red pen.

Running order

    The papersEach folded paper shows the top half of a front page: masthead, headline, the photo and a short piece beside it. The real sentences are the headline’s facts; everything else is blacked out, so it reads in a glance.

    Your pagesYour crawl and your graphs come as open pages, so the map and the chart are full width. The chart keeps the red pen’s three swings; the race under it is everyone, 19:00 to 00:44.

    The endYour profile lands folded, shows your mugshot and who you were to Bob, then opens to a full page: five achievements as stories in two columns, and the motive filed against you.

    Award ideas

    27 awards, each a headline with the winner’s mugshot

    Any number of players can win an award, ties included, and each one prints as a story: the award as the kicker, a headline written for tonight, the mugshot. A player with five awards gets five stories. “Data being added” means the photo tags, facts and video alibis now being built; Whore reads the booking desk’s relation, Sidepiece. Only the stranger tick on an alibi is new.

    B

    The late final

    The whole paper, the same for everyone: a front page, a story per milestone, two honours pages with an article per award, sport, and your clippings

    Moves · each page turns over the spine. Look · the Evening Post from the case-closed set: newsprint, black-and-white prints, tabloid headlines.

    Running order

      The ideaThe night as the next morning’s paper, in page order. The awards get the tabloid treatment: every award is its own story with the winner’s mugshot, two pages of them.

      What’s goodEveryone reads the same paper, so the table can talk about page 10 together. Headlines are the most shareable thing in the set (“Bob’s secret lover named”). Your clippings close it.

      Cost / riskThirteen pages is long. Every headline needs a rule for ties and gaps. The page turn is a 3D transform, cheap on phones, but the back of the page must be drawn.

      C

      Extra! Extra!

      Only your night and the murderer’s: front pages spin in, about you or “meanwhile” about Jonah, with your score against his under each

      Moves · each front page spins in and lands with a thud. Look · black backdrop, one paper at a time, red “meanwhile” editions.

      Running order

        The ideaThe old film montage. You only see what happened to you, and, cut in between, what the killer was up to. It ends with your headlines and a duel chart: your night against his.

        What’s goodPersonal and quick. The duel gives the night a plot (“you passed him for good at 22:35”). Nothing about the other four, so nothing to wade through.

        Cost / riskIf you are the murderer, the “meanwhile” pages need someone else (the runner-up in the verdict). Spinning is loud; with reduced motion it cuts. The others’ nights show only in the final table.

        D

        Rolling news

        The whole night on one clock: the standings race runs from 19:00 to 00:44, a breaking headline drops in at each milestone, and the honours roll like credits

        Moves · the clock runs; headlines drop in word by word; a ticker crawls along the bottom. Look · the Post’s live page: a red live dot, the league table up top.

        Running order

          The ideaA live blog played back. The table is always on screen, so you see every lead change, Ruth’s dive and Jonah’s collapse as they happened, with the news that caused them.

          What’s goodThe standings animation is the whole point, and it never leaves. Short. Tap a chapter to jump the clock. The credits roll every award with its mugshot.

          Cost / riskLittle room for photos and words. Your own night is one row among six. Busy at the hour marks, when lots happens at once.

          E

          The newsstand

          A front page for every suspect: the A-board shows their headline, then their paper comes off the stack with every award they won as a headline and mugshot

          Moves · the vendor swaps the bill, then the paper rises off the stack. Look · a street at night, the A-board’s wire grille, a front page per suspect.

          Running order

            The ideaOrganised by person. Least suspected first, then up the verdict tally, so the last bill on the board is the murderer’s. Yours sits in its place, marked “you”.

            What’s goodEveryone gets a front page; the more you won, the busier it is. Each page carries a sparkline of that player’s night and who they were to Bob.

            Cost / riskFifteen pages. The story milestones only appear in passing. Six front pages need six layouts that cope with one award or seven.
