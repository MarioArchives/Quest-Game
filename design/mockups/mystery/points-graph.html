A

Red penFirst draft

First draft, as recommended in round one. One step chart on squared chart paper: time across, points up. The red pen marks now, the story beats and the swings

Moves · drag along the chart for a ruler; tap a name to follow that line. Look · chart paper, ink for you, pencil-brown for the rest.

The ideaThe chart anyone would draw: a step line per suspect on the paper the Standings page already uses, the bands of the two standings behind it.

What’s goodEasiest to read exact heights and who is ahead at any moment. The axis covers the whole planned night, so it never rescales; the hours still to come hold the labels.

Cost / riskLines bunch where scores are close (Theo and Maya, Priya and Ruth), and crossing lines need a tap to tell apart. Little new code: one SVG chart, a ruler and a card.

At ten playersStill readable: the labels dodge apart in the hours to come, but the middle of the chart turns into a braid. Following one name is the way to read it.

B

Polygraph

A channel per suspect on paper that runs left; the needles write now at the right edge. The examiner numbers each story beat on the chart

Moves · drag the paper back; tap a channel to open it. Look · chart paper in lanes, a steel needle per suspect.

The ideaA lie detector reading of the night: each suspect gets a channel, and the needles move as the points come in.

What’s goodLines never cross, so ten players read as easily as six. The most in-world of the five: the needles and the drag-back paper feel like a prop.

Cost / riskEach lane is about 74 px for 50 points, so small steps (+2) are a few pixels; comparing two suspects means comparing two lanes. Needles and an expanding lane are a medium build.

At ten playersThe lanes shrink to 45 px and stay clean; the scores by the needles carry the exact values.

C

The line-up

Who stood where as each chapter opened: a rank chart on the line-up wall. Each dot carries the score then and wears the standing

Moves · tap a dot for what changed since the last chapter. Look · the line-up wall’s numbered rules, rubber-stamp dots.

The ideaThe night as a series of line-ups. Rank is the story people tell at the bar (“Theo led until the alibis”), so the chart tells that.

What’s goodThe cleanest picture of overtaking, and every dot is an exact number. The standings show as filled dots, no bands needed.

Cost / riskOnly one reading per chapter, so a swing inside a chapter is invisible (Jonah’s −15 between 22:40 and 23:00 shows as one step). Ties and the first chapter (everyone on 0) need rules.

At ten playersTen rows of 41 px still fit; the paths cross more often but stay traceable because each dot is labelled.

D

Down the board

Today’s Standings axis, suspicion left and clear right, with the night running down the page. Each suspect’s string is pinned where the score moved and hangs a tag at now

Moves · tap a string to follow it; tap a pin for its ledger line. Look · the Standings paper, twine strings, kraft tags.

The ideaKeep the Standings axis everyone already reads and give it a time dimension: every face walked a path down the board to where it is now.

What’s goodNo new mental model for left and right; vertical time scrolls like the feed and fits a tall phone; beat names fit on one line in the margin.

Cost / riskTime running down is less familiar than across, and the night outgrows the screen after about 23:30 (scroll or squeeze). The tag row needs dodging when scores bunch.

At ten playersStrings braid where scores bunch near zero; three rows of tags keep the names apart.

E

Watch log

Today’s bars stay; under them, a stakeout log: a row per suspect, a cell per quarter hour, shaded by where the score stood. Drag along the log and the bars rewind

Moves · drag across the log; the bars above jump to that quarter hour. Look · a typed log sheet, red to teal in steps of five.

The ideaSurveillance notes: who was clear and who was under suspicion, quarter hour by quarter hour. The bars stay the place for exact numbers.

What’s goodThe densest of the five: ten suspects in 170 px. The standings read straight off the colour (darkest cells), and rewinding the bars is a nice trick.

Cost / riskColour bands, not heights: “+6” and “+10” look alike, and a quarter hour can hide a swing (Sam’s +22 at 23:50 is gone by 23:58). Heatmaps are the least familiar form for a party crowd.

At ten playersThe easiest at ten: the log just gains rows; the bars above get thinner, as they do today.

Round one’s recommendation: A, red pen, in both places

Chosen with three changes (see Red pen, as chosen at the top): on the Case closed page only, without the small numbers, and with the biggest swings kept.

It is the graph you asked for: everyone’s path on one set of axes, so who is ahead, by how much and since when reads without learning anything. The Now · The night switch adds it without taking the bars away, the ruler gives everyone’s exact score at once, and the Case closed page is the same chart over the whole night, so nobody meets a second kind of picture at 00:44. It is also the smallest build: a pure helper that turns ledger lines into steps, one SVG component, the ruler and its card.

Worth borrowing: E’s rewind (dragging A’s ruler could swing the bars on the Now side to that minute). C is the runner-up for the Case closed page, where chapters as columns tell the story best, but it drops everything that happens between chapters. B is the most fun to look at, but its lanes are short and make two suspects hard to compare.
