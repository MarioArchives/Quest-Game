The verdict

The case closes on the most-incriminated suspect: the one with the most votes on the incriminating photos that tag them.

Frames, upheld charges, failed police check-ins, a missing alibi and standing are shown beside it as also on file, and never decide it.

  1. 1Take the entries to Incriminating photo, each with the players it tags, and the vote tally that closed at the reveal.
  2. 2For every suspect, add up the votes of every photo that tags them. A photo tagging two counts for both.
  3. 3The highest total takes it. Tied: all of them, named as accomplices. No tags, or no votes at all: an open verdict.
  4. 4Store it as facts/verdict (uids and totals) when the GM closes the case, so the pages and the achievements all read one answer.

A

The case fileRecommended

A manila folder you page through, a typed sheet per page, a thumb index of all eleven on its edge

Moves · swipe or tap a tab in the thumb index. Look · typed sheets in the folder; stamps for canon, ties and the charge.

The ideaThe night as the police file it: one typed sheet per fact, clipped in a folder, with every page one tap away on the thumb index.

What’s goodIt is the look you already chose for Missions and My file, so it needs no new parts. Paging is plain and the index shows how much is left. Ties and gaps sit inside one sheet without changing the shape.

Cost / riskThe quietest of the five: the reveal is a stamp, not a moment. Sheets are dense; on a small phone the motives page needs its own scroll.

B

The Didsbury Evening Post

A late final printed when the case closes: a front page, then a story on each inside page

Moves · swipe to turn pages; tap the index on the front. Look · newsprint, black-and-white prints, tabloid headlines, a league table.

The ideaThe night as the next morning’s paper. Each fact is a story with a headline, so the copy carries the jokes and the data fills the captions.

What’s goodHeadlines are the most shareable thing on this page (“IT WAS JONAH”). A league table reads standings at a glance. Ties and gaps become news (“police find two”, “still missing”).

Cost / riskThe most writing: every headline needs a rule for each case (tie, gap, open verdict) or it reads wrong. Newsprint is a new look for the pack.

C

Court in session

A trial over the whole screen: each fact is an exhibit called before the jury, and the jury’s votes give the verdict

Moves · tap for the next exhibit; bars on top count them. Look · the court’s felt and wood, evidence bags, a typed transcript.

The ideaThe recap as the trial nobody held: each fact is called as an exhibit, and the verdict really does come from a vote, so “the jury” is the group.

What’s goodThe verdict lands hardest here: a jury slip read out after seven exhibits. Evidence bags carry who entered what and the votes on one label. Full screen makes it an event.

Cost / riskFull screen hides the tabs, so it needs its own way out (the ×) and a way back in from Case file. Felt and wood are new surfaces for the pack.

D

The solution

Cluedo’s envelope: a card for each fact, and a sentence on top that fills in, slot by slot, until it names the culprit

Moves · swipe card to card; the strip on top is the progress. Look · playing cards on chart paper, one colour per kind of card.

The ideaEveryone knows how Cluedo ends: “Jonah, in the cellar, with the corkscrew”. Each page deals one card, and the strip on top builds that sentence as you go.

What’s goodThe payoff is one card that says the whole night in a sentence, ready to save and share. Ties show as two cards in one slot; gaps as a blank card. The notepad makes the vote count visible before the verdict.

Cost / riskCards are small on a phone, so motives and losing entries get cramped. The strip takes 60 px on every page. Card art is a new set of parts.

E

The evidence board

One big board of everything pinned up tonight; each page pans and zooms to a corner of it, and the red string ends at the culprit

Moves · swipe to the next corner; pinch to roam; the minimap shows where you are. Look · prints, cards and tape on the wall, red and green string.

The ideaThe detective’s wall from every crime drama. All the night’s evidence lives on one board, and the recap is a guided walk across it.

What’s goodThe verdict is visible before it is announced: you can see the strings pile up on one mugshot. Roaming the board is fun after the walk. An open verdict is easy to read: slack grey string.

Cost / riskThe hardest to build: a pannable board, string drawn between moving parts, a minimap. Text on the board is small, so the caption card does the reading.

Recommendation: A, the case file, with D’s solution card as its verdict page

A is the look you already chose for Missions and My file, so it needs the fewest new parts, and paging a folder with a thumb index is the plainest way through eleven pages. It is quiet at the end, though, so swap its charge sheet (page 9) for D’s solution card: the whole night in one sentence, the six cards under it, and a button to save it as an image. That is the page people will share.

Worth borrowing: D’s “envelope so far” line at the foot of A’s pages 3–8, so the sentence builds as you page; B’s headlines for the cover; E’s string thickness as the chart on the verdict page. Keep C’s full screen in mind if you want the reveal to feel like an event: it suits a GM reading it out to the table.
