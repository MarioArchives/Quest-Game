A

Case cards

A full-width case card per live mission, every detail and the button on it; the sections below are a typed list

Banner · one big card, the next one’s edge showing. Sections · roomy two-line rows, oldest first, every state in words.

The ideaThe banner does the work: every live mission is a complete case card you can act on without leaving the top of the tab. The sections are the record.

What’s goodNothing to open: what, how long, who’s in and the button are all on the card. The list is plain to read and to build (today’s side-quest card, restyled).

Cost / riskThe cards are tall (about 330 px), so the sections start below the fold on a small phone. A live mission shows twice, card and row, and their states must agree.

B

Tags on the line

Live missions hang as tags on a twine line across the folder; below, each kind is a track of the night, start to finish

Banner · three tags on a line, the front one readable. Sections · a track per kind: filed, live, next and sealed as stations.

The ideaPhysical and glanceable: the number of strings is the number of live missions, and every kind of mission is a line you travel along through the night.

What’s goodThe tracks show the shape of the night at a glance: what’s done, what’s live, when the next roll call is, how much story is left. Compact, so all three kinds fit one screen.

Cost / riskThe most new drawing of the five (twine, tags, tracks). Track labels are tiny; seven story stations is the most a phone fits. Tag titles must stay short.

C

Stop press

One typed line at a time, pinned under the folder tabs on every tab; below it, the whole night as a dense ledger

Banner · a ticker strip that turns every few seconds or on a swipe. Sections · one ruled line per mission; sealed chapters redacted.

The ideaTake the least space for “what’s live” and give the rest to the record. The ticker never scrolls away, on any folder tab.

What’s goodThe most of the night on one screen; the whole of each kind at a glance, with times. Redacted chapters are suspense for free. The cheapest banner to build.

Cost / riskOne line per live mission says little: to act, you tap through. A ticker that turns by itself moves under your thumb (stop it on touch; never under reduced motion). Small type.

D

Evidence film

Live missions as frames on a strip of film clipped to the folder; below, each kind is a contact sheet of the night’s photos

Banner · a film strip, the clock printed in the corner like a date stamp. Sections · prints: what was shot, what’s live, what’s still dark.

The ideaThe game is photos, so show them: what a mission wants, what others did, what you sent. The night reads as a roll of film.

What’s goodThe most in-world and the most fun to scroll back through. Your own answers stay with their roll calls. Film numbers link a print to its frame with no extra words.

Cost / riskNeeds photos to look right: early in the night, or for text-only answers, it is a sheet of blanks. Hidden entries can’t be shown, so a drop’s frame is yours or a sketch. Image-heavy on a pub’s signal.

E

The stack

Live missions as a stack of folders inside the folder, each with its own tab; the sections are index dividers, filed missions folded away

Banner · a stack of folders: the front one open, the others’ tabs showing. Sections · what’s open first, filed folded shut, a thumb index on the edge.

The ideaFolders all the way down: the Missions folder holds a stack of mission folders, so A’s look carries into the banner, and every live mission is one tab away.

What’s goodThe tabs say what’s live and how long each has before you swipe. Filed missions stay out of the way, so the sections are about what’s open and next. Short sections, quick to scan.

Cost / riskFolders inside a folder could feel like a tab too many. More than four live at once crowds the tabs (a fifth would need a “+2”). The thumb index takes 28 px of width.

Recommendation: E, the stack

E is A’s folder carried all the way in: the banner is more folders, so the look you chose holds from the tabs down, and the stack’s own tabs show what’s live and how long each has before you swipe. The front folder carries the whole mission and its button. Below it, folding filed missions away keeps the three sections about what’s open and what’s next, and they stay short.

Worth borrowing: B’s tracks for Periodic and Story, if you want the shape of the night (four roll calls, seven chapters) at a glance; C’s redacted titles for sealed chapters; D’s date-stamp clock. If a stack inside a folder feels like one layer too many, A is the plain version of the same idea.
