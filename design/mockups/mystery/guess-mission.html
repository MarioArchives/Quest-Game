Guessing a suspect’s tampering mission: five ways to make an accusation

What’s being decided

On another player’s file, the accusation tag opens today’s guess panel: one long list of all 68 tampering missions with a search box, tap to read, select, then “Make guess”.

What it lacks: the list scrolls inside a small box, nothing says which missions can’t be his, your earlier misses aren’t shown, and the result is a toast.

Each option replaces that panel. Every row shows the same five moments: opening it on Jonah’s file, finding a mission among 68, confirming, and both results.

The rules, as built (guessQuest, mystery settings)

  • Pick fromall 68 tampering missions, not just his.
  • Guesses3 for the whole game, right or wrong, on anyone.
  • Cool-downone guess per suspect every 10 min.
  • Righthe holds it and hasn’t sent it to a witness yet: you +5, he −5. A Board post says Priya exposed his secret mission, “Hold this”. It leaves his hand and he’s dealt a fresh one.
  • Wrongyou −5. No post, and he isn’t told: only you and the GM can read your guesses.
  • Never twicenobody is dealt a mission they’ve held, so the ones he’s done (posted on the Board) are ruled out for good. A wrong guess only means “not his at 20:55”.

A

Charge sheet

A typed police form: Suspect, Charge, Signed. The Charge line is a search that types back.

68 missions · type any word from a mission and matches drop under the line, with related words beneath; an empty line is the whole book A–Z.

The ideaThe accusation as a typed police form. The Charge line is a search: type any word you remember and the matching missions drop under it.

What’s goodFastest when you remember a word: a tap and a few letters. Needs no new data, since it searches titles and text as today. The stamp and the “On file” list make each guess feel like an event.

Cost / riskSearch only finds the words a mission uses, so “pint” misses Hold this without the “Words like it” line. With the keyboard up only three or four results show. With no word in mind it’s a plain A–Z list.

B

Charge book

The whole catalogue as an indexed book: who-was-involved chips, search, and what can’t be his filed at the back.

68 missions · chips by who’s involved, letter headers that stick, a letter rail, your guesses on top and the ruled-out folded away at the end.

The ideaToday’s list, made into an indexed book: chips for who was involved, letters that stick, your guesses kept on top and what can’t be his filed at the back.

What’s goodNothing is hidden: every mission is a scroll away, so no filter can lose the answer. It separates what’s certain (done or exposed, never dealt again) from what’s stale (your miss at 20:55). Closest to today’s guess panel.

Cost / riskNeeds a “who” tag on each of the 68 missions in quests.json. A chip can mislead unless “Anyone” missions show under both Player and Stranger. Still the most scrolling of the five.

C

Suspect deck

The missions as a deck of index cards: swipe right to suspect, left to rule out, then accuse from a shortlist.

68 missions · the 2 he’s done are never dealt, a chip or a search deals a smaller deck, and the shortlist and ruled-out pile are kept per suspect.

The ideaSift the missions one card at a time: right to suspect, left to rule out, then accuse from a short list.

What’s goodOne-thumbed and fun at the bar, and every mission gets read properly. The shortlist and the ruled-out pile become a memory for the whole night.

Cost / risk66 cards is a lot of swiping unless you start from a chip or a search. The shortlist and pile need saving per suspect (on the phone, or a new private collection). Swipes need button twins for accessibility.

D

What did you see?

Two quick questions about what you saw Jonah do, and the 68 narrow to the few that fit.

68 missions · each mission carries clue tags; every answer shows its count first (66 › 35 › 2), and “Show all” is always one tap away.

The ideaStart from what you saw. Two questions (who else, what was involved) narrow 68 to a handful, and each answer shows how many it leaves.

What’s goodQuickest from a sighting to a pick, with no typing, and it teaches players what to watch for. The running counts make the odds visible.

Cost / riskEvery mission needs clue tags (who and what), and a wrong tag hides the right answer, so “Show all” stays one tap away. Vague sightings need tags that overlap (“someone” counts as a player and a stranger).

E

Case notes

Private notes on Jonah through the night; each note strings itself to the missions it could be.

68 missions · your notes do the narrowing (their words match mission text), the board shows only pinned leads, and the pin drawer searches all 66.

The ideaKeep private notes on Jonah as the night goes. Each note strings itself to the missions that share its words, and you accuse the card with the most string.

What’s goodMatches how people play: you see something at 21:12 and accuse at 22:00. The most in-world of the five, and the notes help with every suspect, not just one guess.

Cost / riskThe biggest build: notes need storing (on the phone, or a new owner-only collection with rules and tests) plus word matching. Players who never write a note only get the search drawer.

Recommendation: B, the charge book, with D’s questions as its chips

B is the only option where nothing can hide the right mission: the whole book stays a scroll away, what can’t be his is filed at the back, and your misses stay struck through on top. It is also the closest to today’s guess panel, so it’s the cheapest to build well.

Its one new piece of data, a tag per mission, is what D needs too. Put D’s two questions on the book as its chip rows (who else, what was involved) with their counts, and it narrows like D without D’s risk. Borrow A’s “Words like it” for the search (“pint” finds Hold this) and A’s stamped result. E’s private notes are the best next feature, but a feature of their own.
