Recommendation: D, the stack

D is the only style that grows out of what the app already does. The Missions tab is a stack of folders you flick to the back, and D’s folder tabs shuffle the folder with the same flick, so the night teaches one gesture. Changing section lands a new folder on top with a 6 % drop and a shadow, which is quick and has almost no travel, so it stays pleasant on the fortieth tap. Folder tabs get the clear left and right that a row of tabs suggests, and reduced motion simply switches it off.

It costs a little more than A: the drop is plain View Transitions CSS, the shuffle needs a small wrapper (about 90 lines) in Board and GmLocker. If that is too much for now, ship A everywhere first; it is the same wiring, and D’s folder shuffle can follow later. B is the runner-up for its put-it-away “back”, but its full-height rise is too much for the bottom tabs. C and E are the most fun to watch and the costliest to keep smooth.

Wiring, whichever style

Where it plugs into the app (AppFrame, Board, GmLocker)

  • Section or folder?Compare litTab() before and after. A different bottom tab is a section change; the same one is a folder change. Missions ↔ Map changes the route (/map) but stays a folder change.
  • Which way?The orders the app already has: tabsFor() for the bottom tabs, Missions · Map · Standings in Board, LOCKER_TABS in GmLocker. Further along is forwards.
  • A tap mid-changeA new startViewTransition skips the running one, and ::view-transition { pointer-events: none } lets the tap through to the new screen. The wrappers finish their animations first. Either way the second tab wins at once.
  • Reduced motionKeyframes sit inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference); the wrappers check matchMedia. Folder tabs with arrow keys follow the same rule.
  • Timing as tokens--tab-ms and --folder-ms in the pack’s theme.css, so another pack can slow it down or switch it off without touching the engine.
  • Scroll and focusAppFrame’s scroll-to-top and focus move stay as they are. The old screen is captured before the scroll, so it leaves from where you were.
