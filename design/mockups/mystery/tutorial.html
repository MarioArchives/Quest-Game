Mystery Pub Crawl · design decision · the tutorial

How does a new suspect learn the app?

A player finishes the booking desk and lands on a sealed case file with nothing to do until 19:00. Nothing in the app explains the Missions folder, the log book’s stamps, the witness, the reward, charges or the police. Five ways to teach it: each covers every section, shows where it is offered and how to find it again mid-game.

What every option teaches
  • MissionsGroup missions: a photo each, then a yes/no vote; most yeses +5, skipping −5. Story missions from the GM. Roll call every hour, −5 if missed.
  • MapThe route’s bars stay sealed until the GM reveals them, one at a time.
  • StandingsAbove +10 a trusted confidant; below −10 a person of interest.
  • Log bookCredible or Doubtful, one stamp per post, never your own; the most credible is pinned on top; no points.
  • My fileFive secret tampering missions with a Proof: line; a witness confirms (+5) or rejects (−5, fresh one); rerolls; your character and secret motive.
  • RewardAfter a confirmed mission: destroy (+5 to you) or frame (−5 to them, anonymous).
  • ChargesGuess a suspect’s mission from their file: right +5 (they lose 5), wrong −5. Three a game.
  • PolicePersons of interest: a Telegram call every 30 min (+5 / −2 / −5). Confidants: one tip-off an hour. Case updates in the police chat.

A

The induction briefing

DI Margaret Hollings talks you through the night in six short slides, each with an exhibit: a print of the real screen, ringed in red pen.

Full screen after the booking desk · replay from My file

Good

One voice, in order: the police fiction of the booking desk carries straight on, and nobody skips a section by accident.

Cost

Linear and passive: people tap Next without reading, and finding one answer mid-game means paging to the right slide.

Build

Six static slides in the pack, exhibits drawn with the case-file kit, one “seen” flag per player. Small.

B

The torch tour

The real tabs, filled with a specimen night, go dark; a torch picks out one thing at a time and a tag on twine says what it does.

Over the real tabs, while you wait · replay from My file

Good

It teaches the real screens in place, so the tabs already look familiar when the case opens.

Cost

Before 19:00 the tabs are empty, so it needs a specimen night of fake missions and posts, and every screen change can break a stop. Small type under a scrim is hard to read in a pub.

Build

A specimen game state, a spotlight layer over the app frame, and an anchor on each real element it points at. Medium to large.

C

The suspect’s handbook

A pocket rule book issued with your file: one printed page per part of the app, a thumb index down the edge, read in order or dipped into later.

Issued at the end of the booking desk · kept in My file

Good

A reference people can dip into mid-game; the thumb index and the § marks reach any rule in one tap, and the text is easy to keep in step with the rules.

Cost

It is reading. Few people read a handbook before a night out, and the figures are drawings, not the screens they will see.

Build

Seven static pages and a § link on a few headings. The cheapest of the five.

D

The dry run

A practice case before the real one: you do a mission, get a vote, charge a suspect and take a police call, with Desk Sergeant Cottam and Cardboard Colin. Nothing is scored.

Offered while you wait · rerun from My file

Good

People learn by doing: after one practice submit, vote, stamp, charge and call, the real ones hold no surprises.

Cost

Ten minutes is a lot straight after the booking desk. Map, standings, story missions, roll call and tip-offs are only told, not drilled. The practice must never leak into the real game.

Build

Heaviest: a practice copy of the game in the browser (the mock’s in-memory store could host it), two fake suspects and a fake call. Large.

E

The evidence wall

Recommended: Eight index cards pinned to a cork wall and joined by red twine in reading order. Tap a card for one screen on that part of the app; read them all, or just the one you need.

Pinned under the sealed case file · a ? on each tab later · kept in My file
Recommended

Good

The whole app on one screen, and each card is one screen, so it is as short or as long as you want. Mid-game, the ? opens just the card for the tab you are on.

Cost

Not linear: some people will open two cards and stop. The twine, the Seen stamps and the “3 of 8” count nudge them on.

Build

Eight static cards with small exhibits from the case-file kit, the cork from the vote board, and a “read” set per player. Small to medium.

My pick: E

E, the evidence wall, because it is the only one that works both before the night and during it. While the case file is sealed it shows a new suspect the whole app on one screen and lets them read as much as they like; mid-game, the ? on a tab opens just the card they need, which the slides, the tour and the dry run can’t do. It needs no specimen night and no practice state, so it is cheap to build and stays right when a screen changes.

Worth borrowing: A’s DI Hollings as the voice of the cards (each one signed); B’s single spotlight the first time something real happens (the first group mission, the first witness request), pointing back to its card; C’s § numbers, so a card and a rule can be quoted.
