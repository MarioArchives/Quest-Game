How should a player’s standing show on their folder?

Today it’s a small teal or red stamp under the name. Here are five ways to make it a tag on the suspect folder itself. Each row shows the same four files, so every state is visible side by side, plus the live tags: an open police check-in and your own tip-off timer.

Note: police check-ins are private to the player and the GM today. Showing “check-in open” on someone else’s file needs a public flag on the player.

The rules, as built

  • Trusted confidant: score above +10. Can tip off the police about another player once every 60 min.
  • Person of interest: score below −10. Gets a police check-in every 30 min while it lasts.
  • No standing: anything from −10 to +10 (exactly ±10 counts as none). The GM moves both lines in Settings.
  • Police check-in: a sum (“What’s 7 × 8?”) answered within 5 min. Right +5, wrong or none −5.
A

Index tabs

Clip-on plastic tabs on the folder’s top edge, one colour per status, like a filing system.

The idea

A coloured index tab clips onto the folder’s top edge beside the name tab: teal for a trusted confidant, red for a person of interest, clear for no standing. A live state (police, tip ready) stands on a taller divider behind it.

Good

Reads like a real filing drawer: colour and words, never on top of the file’s contents. The same tab shrinks to a coloured tab on the Board.

Cost / risk

The top edge is short: labels go to two lines at 10px, and a live state has to stand on a divider behind (Jonah, My file), so the file sits about 20px lower. No room for the rule (above +10) on the tab.

B

Evidence tag on the clip

A kraft tag tied to the folder’s paperclip and stamped in teal or red ink.

The idea

The paperclip on the tab holds a kraft tag on twine, stamped TRUSTED CONFIDANT or PERSON OF INTEREST, with the rule under it. A red police tag (or your tip timer) is tied on beside it.

Good

Pure case-file kit (eyelet, twine, ink stamp), so it matches the strung tags further down. Room for the rule on the tag; more tags just fan out from the clip.

Cost / risk

The tags lie in the space above the folder, so the file starts about 36px lower (86 on My file, under the REC strip). Tilted tags on twine get busy, and the type is small.

C

Classification band

A coloured band across the top of the folder: CLEARED or WATCH LIST.

The idea

A full-width band across the top of the file: teal CLEARED for a trusted confidant, red WATCH LIST for a person of interest, a dashed UNCLASSIFIED strip for no standing. An open check-in adds police tape under it.

Good

The loudest and clearest: full words plus the rule, readable at arm’s length. On My file a tear-off stub carries the tip-off state.

Cost / risk

Pushes the file’s contents down about 45px (75 with the police tape). Feels printed rather than stuck on, and Jonah’s file gets heavy with two bars.

D

Sticky flags on the side

Index flags stuck behind the folder, poking out of its right edge.

The idea

Sticky index flags poke out of the folder’s right edge: teal, red or kraft for the standing, then ink for an open police check-in and kraft for your tip timer, stacked down the edge.

Good

Never touches the file’s contents, and any number of live states stack down the edge. The colours show even when files are piled up on the Board.

Cost / risk

Sideways text is slower to read, and the folder loses 16px of width. No room for the rule, and the flags are too thin to tap if they ever need to be buttons.

E

Colour-coded file

The folder stock and its tab change with standing; live states are dot stickers.

The idea

The whole folder changes: a teal-green file titled TRUSTED CONFIDANT, plain manila for no standing, a red PERSON OF INTEREST file. A typed line gives the rule; police and tip states are round dot stickers on the corner.

Good

The strongest at-a-glance signal, from across a pub table. The tab names the standing in words, and nothing new is drawn on top of the file.

Cost / risk

Two new folder colours to tune, and files stop all being manila. The Board, feed and standings should use the same colours to stay consistent.
