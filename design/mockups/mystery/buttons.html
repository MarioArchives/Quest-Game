Mystery Pub Crawl · buttons · colour

Buttons off teal: five colour schemes, teal kept for “in the clear”

What’s being decided

The mystery pack’s buttons are teal (--accent #17736f, white letters): Enter on the Missions folder, Let in and Confirm on the GM’s tags, the evidence sheet’s main button, the booking desk’s go button.

Teal also means in the clear: the standings’ bars and the trusted-confidant pin. So buttons get tokens of their own (--btn-*) and teal is left to the standings. Below are five schemes for those tokens, each with the full set: primary, secondary, quiet, destructive, selected, disabled, busy, hover, press and focus.

Contrast is WCAG 2 (AA: 4.5:1 for button letters). Measured on manila, kraft, paper, chart paper, the charcoal wall and the booking room.

Case brownRecommended

Your idea: dark brown with manila letters, the GM’s Fire button made the house button

#3a2e1c · #e6cf98 · letters 8.7:1

The ideaThe Fire button you already like, made every button’s colour: case-file brown (#3a2e1c) with manila letters, a near-black edge and the hard paper shadow. Secondary is the same brown as a 2 px rule; selected is the brown filled with a tick.

What’s goodLetters at 8.7:1 everywhere, the highest of the five. Brown belongs to the folders’ own inks, so it reads as part of the case file rather than a colour laid on top, and teal is left meaning “in the clear”. It is already in the code as gl-btn--ink.

Cost / riskOn the charcoal wall the brown is only 1.3:1 against it, so it needs the kraft rim there (the letters still read without it). Next to the manila-ink text on folders a brown button is quieter than teal was: it relies on its fill and shadow, not on colour, to be found.

Busy · destructiveBusy keeps the full brown and draws the bee’s line in manila, so the gold body sits on dark (6.5:1). Destructive stays red: a red rule (#74231c, 4.7:1 on kraft) for Reject and Close, solid red only on the confirm sheet.

Every state on every surface · Aa = the letters against what’s behind them (AA needs 4.5) · ◻ = the button against the surface (3 is the bar for a shape that has to be seen) · bee = its gold body against the fill

Oxblood leather

A deep oxblood with cream letters and a stitched inner line, like a leather case binder

#6a2620 · #f4ecd5 · letters 9.3:1

The ideaButtons as oxblood leather, the colour of an old case binder: #6a2620 with cream letters and a dashed cream stitch just inside the edge. Secondary drops to an ink-brown rule, so oxblood only ever means “go”.

What’s goodRich and warm, the most “object” of the five; the stitch gives it texture without a pattern. Cream on oxblood is 9.3:1, and the gold bee reads on it (5.4:1).

Cost / riskIt is a red. On the Board, the standings’ red bars mean under suspicion, and on the reward the Frame stamp is red, so Destroy can’t take the button colour (it goes ink). Colour-blind players see oxblood and danger red as two dark browns.

Busy · destructiveDestructive moves away from the fill: a red rule with a ✕ on paper, and a brighter red (#b53a2e) with a ✕ in the confirm sheet. Busy keeps the stitch and draws the bee in cream.

Every state on every surface · Aa = the letters against what’s behind them (AA needs 4.5) · ◻ = the button against the surface (3 is the bar for a shape that has to be seen) · bee = its gold body against the fill

Rubber stamp

No fill: a double-ruled brown stamp in Archivo Black, pressed slightly crooked; pressing inks it solid

transparent · #3e2a16 · letters 8.9:1

The ideaEvery button is a rubber stamp in brown ink (#3e2a16): the main action a double rule with Archivo Black letters, set slightly crooked like the case stamps; secondary a single rule in mono. Pressing it inks it solid, so the tap lands like a stamp.

What’s goodThe most in-world of the five, and no colour at all to clash with the standings or the red. Letters are 6.1:1 on kraft and 8.9:1 on manila; the same rules work on the wall in manila-tab (5.7:1).

Cost / riskA hollow primary is the weakest call to action: on a kraft tag full of type, “Restore lines” is easy to miss, and primary and secondary differ only by a second rule and the face. Archivo Black is wide, so pairs need smaller letters (10.5 px).

Busy · destructiveDestructive is the same stamp in red with a ✕ (4.7:1 on kraft); the confirm sheet inks it solid. Busy inks the stamp solid, as a press does, so the gold bee sits on brown (6.6:1; on the wall it stays hollow) and it can’t be stamped twice; disabled is a dashed, un-inked rule.

Every state on every surface · Aa = the letters against what’s behind them (AA needs 4.5) · ◻ = the button against the surface (3 is the bar for a shape that has to be seen) · bee = its gold body against the fill

Saddle key

Two-tone: a saddle-brown face on a darker brown edge, a key you press down; secondary a cream key

#6e4220 · #f4ecd5 · letters 7.2:1

The ideaA two-tone key: a saddle-brown face (#6e4220) on a darker brown edge (#3a2412) that you press down into, with a little give at the top. Secondary is a cream key on the same edge; selected is a key locked down, dark and sunk.

What’s goodThe clearest “this is pressable” of the five: the edge does the work on every paper colour, and press feels like press. Cream on saddle is 7.2:1. Mid-brown sits between the folders and the inks, so it is warm without being loud.

Cost / riskIt brings depth (rounded corners, an edge, a highlight) into a look that is otherwise flat paper with hard shadows, so it can feel like a different app. The saddle face is close to kraft (3.8:1), so on tags it leans on the edge. The wall needs a kraft rim.

Busy · destructiveBusy holds the key down (it can’t be pressed again) with the bee in cream. Destructive is a red key on a darker red edge; Reject and Close are cream keys lettered in red (7.1:1).

Every state on every surface · Aa = the letters against what’s behind them (AA needs 4.5) · ◻ = the button against the surface (3 is the bar for a shape that has to be seen) · bee = its gold body against the fill
