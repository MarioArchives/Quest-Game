Five ways to hold your tampering missions

Today every mission is a kraft tag strung under the last, in the order dealt. You’re dealt five; a done one keeps its place, and an exposed one stays on the line while a fresh one is dealt, so by mid-evening it’s six or more tags: about two screens of scrolling before Exhibits and the police line. The ask: Perhaps it’s better if it is in a carousel of tags or with tabs like the rest of the page. Here are both, and three more.

A

On the line

A tag carousel: the twine runs across the page and the tags hang from it side by side. One at a time, swipe along; pegs under the line show the whole hand.

Opens on · news you haven’t seen (a verdict, an exposure) once; otherwise your first to do.

1 screenTodayA

Missions: about 2 screens today, ¾ of a screen here.

The ideaKeep the kraft tag, turn the line sideways. A tag gets the whole width and a fixed height, so the page below stops jumping as states change. The pegs double as page dots and the at-a-glance view.

What’s goodThe closest to today’s look and the most tactile: swiping along a line of tags feels like the case file. Fixed height, so Exhibits and the police line are always the same scroll away. Reroll on the back of the tag is a nice in-world check.

Cost / riskOnly one title is readable at a time, so “which one can I do right now?” means swiping through all of them. A sideways swipe inside a page that scrolls up and down is easy to fumble; the arrows and pegs have to be real buttons for keyboards and screen readers.

B

Binder tabs

The Board’s group-missions binder, on My file: one manila page, a divider tab per mission down its right edge, newest on top. Filed tabs slim down.

Opens on · news you haven’t seen once; otherwise your first to do. A tab you chose stays chosen.

1 screenTodayB

Missions: about 2 screens today, ¾ of a screen here.

The ideaReuse MissionBinder’s look and rules so the two halves of the game read the same: number and state on the tab, the full mission on the page. Submit happens on the page, without a sheet.

What’s goodConsistent with the Board, which players already know. Direct: one tap to any mission, no swiping. The page has room for the whole description, the proof line and the witness picker, so claiming is one screen.

Cost / riskTabs show a number and a state, never a title, so you still open them one by one to remember what No. 5 was. Two binders with different jobs could blur. A long night (a dozen filed) needs the slim tabs to stay short.

C

Split the file

Tabs like the rest of the app at page level: My file gets Suspect, Missions and Exhibits folder tabs, as the Board has Feed and Standings. Missions keep today’s tags, sorted.

Opens on · the tab you used last; first visit, Missions. Witness requests show above the tabs on every tab.

1 screenTodaySuspectMissions

No tab is long any more, but the missions tab is as long as today.

The ideaDon’t shrink the tags; give them a page of their own. The tags themselves barely change, only their order: to do first, then with your witness, then filed ones short.

What’s goodThe smallest change to how missions look, and every word of every mission stays on screen. The suspect file and police line are no longer below two screens of tags. The tab count (3) says how many are left without opening it.

Cost / riskIt adds a level: missions are a tap away from the file, not on it, and the Missions tab still scrolls about two screens. Three tabs on My file plus two on the Board is more chrome for a night out.

D

Case log

A typed index card, one line per mission with the dossier’s own marks [ ] [~] [✓] [x]. Tap a line and its kraft tag slides out under it, with everything you can do.

Opens on · all lines closed, to do first, filed at the bottom. A new verdict or exposure opens its line once.

1 screenTodayD

Missions: about 2 screens today, under 1 screen here with one line open.

The ideaSeparate scanning from doing. The card is for scanning: every title and state in six lines. The tag that slides out is for doing: what it asks, the proof, Submit, Reroll, add a photo.

What’s goodThe only option where every title is readable at once, which is what you need in a pub when a chance turns up (“someone’s getting up: Seat thief!”). Compact, and it grows only by the line you open. Cheap: the [ ] [~] [✓] [x] tags already exist in dossier.ts.

Cost / riskLess of the strung-tag spectacle on the file: tags only appear when opened. Opening a line pushes the rest down, so the card’s height changes as you use it. Long titles get cut to one line when closed.

E

Tucked in your file

The missions become part of the suspect folder: kraft tags tucked inside it, sticking out of its bottom edge like bookmarks. To do ones stick out furthest.

Opens on · nothing open: the stubs are the overview. Tap one to pull it out full size.

1 screenTodayE

Missions: about 2 screens today, a strip under the folder here.

The ideaYour missions are evidence in your own file, so file them there. How far a tag sticks out says its state: out for to do, a bit less for the one with your witness, pushed in once filed.

What’s goodBy far the shortest page: the whole hand costs one strip, so without a witness request it’s on the first screen. The tag pulled out full size is roomy, and the witness as a mugshot print matches the reward and tip-off pickers.

Cost / riskTitles run sideways on the stubs, which is slow to read and cuts long names. Everything takes a tap to open. Only seven to eight stubs fit across a phone; a long night needs filed ones bundled into one stub.

Recommendation: D, the case log

It’s the only layout where you can read all your missions at once, and that is the job My file does mid-game: spotting which mission fits the moment. One typed line each, with the file’s own [ ] [~] [✓] [x] marks, then the kraft tag slides out right there for Submit, Reroll or a photo. It brings the missions up from about two screens to under one without hiding any of them, and it’s the cheapest to build.

Worth borrowing: A’s reroll on the back of the tag (a clearer check than a sheet), B’s witness picked in place with last time’s witness ready, and “a new verdict or exposure opens itself once” from A and B. If matching the Board matters more than reading titles, B is the pick: it’s the binder players already know.
