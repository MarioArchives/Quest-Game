Five ways to choose who to frame

What’s being decided

A witness confirms your claim and the Evidence secured file opens: Destroy (+5 to you) or Frame (−5 off a suspect). Today Frame opens a grid of mugshots, and a single tap frames: no second look, no hint of what −5 does.

Each option replaces that grid: how the suspects are shown, how a slip of the thumb is caught, and what you see when it’s done.

The table in every frame (you: 5 pts, #3)

  • Maya+15→ +10 · loses trusted confidant (it’s above +10)
  • Theo+8→ +3 · drops below you
  • Sam+2→ −3 · under suspicion
  • Ruth−3→ −8 · the pick in every frame
  • Priya−9→ −14 · becomes a person of interest
  • Jonah−15→ −20 · already watched

Rules every option keeps

Anyone still playing except you; players who have left aren’t shown. One reward, one frame, −5.

Anonymous: the feed posts “Anonymous tip: someone framed Ruth”. No player sees who; the GM’s Frames on file does.

One tell: your confirmed claim posts to the feed just before the tip. Closing the pop-up keeps the reward, so you can frame later, once the trail’s cold.

Frames: 1 choose Frame · 2 pick a suspect · 3 confirm · 4 done

A

The line-up

Six suspects against a height chart where height is their score, the ±10 lines painted on the wall

Mis-tap guard
Tap a suspect to step them forward; Frame Ruth is a second button.
Strategy cue
Height is score: the leader stands tallest and the watched stand below the red line.

The ideaA police line-up you pick from. The height chart is the score scale, so the ranking and the two lines are the picture itself.

What’s goodThe most theatrical moment of the night, and still strategic: who’s tall, who’s close to a line and what −5 does all show without reading a number.

Cost / riskSix slots fill the width; past eight players they get thin, so it needs two rows or a sideways scroll. The most drawing to build, and “short means suspicious” needs its hint line.

B

The evidence board

Prints pinned to the wall; the first tap runs your red string to one, the second tap pins it

Mis-tap guard
Tap a print to run the string; tap the same print again, or Pin it on Ruth, to frame.
Strategy cue
The pins are the standings (phone, eye, steel), and the detective’s red notes flag who’s close.

The ideaYour evidence tag on a red string. Tap a face and the string runs to it; tap the same face again and the tag is pinned there.

What’s goodIt feels like the detective’s own work. Tapping the same face twice is a natural guard, and the string shows your pick from anywhere on the screen.

Cost / riskScores are small captions, so strategy is secondary to the scene. A 3 × 2 board fits six, nine at most before it scrolls, and “tap again” needs teaching once.

C

Suspect files

A swipeable deck of manila files, one per suspect, opening on the leader; press and hold the stamp to frame

Mis-tap guard
Press and hold the Frame stamp until the ring closes (about a second). A tap does nothing.
Strategy cue
Each file says what −5 would do to that suspect, in words.

The ideaOne manila file per suspect, swiped like a deck, with a tab strip so all six stay one tap away. You frame by stamping the open file.

What’s goodThe most room per suspect: a big mugshot, their standing and the consequence spelt out. Hold-to-stamp is the strongest guard against a slip of the thumb.

Cost / riskComparing suspects means swiping back and forth, so it is the slowest of the five. Holding needs a visible ring and a tap-and-confirm fallback for screen readers.

D

Plant the evidence

Pick up the Frame tag and drop it on a face; it hangs there for five seconds before it files

Mis-tap guard
A drag can’t happen by accident, and the drop gives you five seconds to take it back.
Strategy cue
Dragging over a face previews its score after −5 before you let go.

The ideaThe choice and the pick are one gesture: you plant the tag on a suspect yourself. An undo slip replaces the confirm question.

What’s goodThe most physical and the quickest. Undo is kinder than “are you sure?”, and ending on the feed proves the promise: the post says someone.

Cost / riskDragging over a small grid is fiddly one-handed in a crowded pub, so it needs tap-to-pick as well. The call goes out late: closing the app inside the five seconds must still file it.

E

The suspect sheet

The Board’s standings re-drawn for the choice: each suspect now and after −5, and what crosses a line

Mis-tap guard
Tapping a row opens an unsigned tip slip; Send it unsigned is the only way to frame.
Strategy cue
Every row shows now and after on the ±10 scale, with a stamp when a line is crossed.

The ideaA strategist’s view: the standings sheet with each suspect’s after-frame position and the consequence stamped on the row.

What’s goodThe only option that answers “who is it worth framing?” outright, including what it does for you. It scrolls to sixteen players and reuses the Board’s chart, so it is the cheapest. The unsigned slip turns the anonymity promise into the confirm step.

Cost / riskThe least theatrical: a lot of reading at a moment of glee. The stamps need careful copy whenever the GM moves the lines.

Recommendation: E, the suspect sheet

Framing is the one strategic choice the reward gives you, and E is the only option that shows its consequences outright: Maya losing confidant, Priya going on the watch list, you passing Theo. It reuses the Board’s standings chart and the GM’s lines, so it stays right when the lines move. It scrolls to any size of party, and the unsigned tip slip makes the anonymity promise part of the confirm step.

Worth borrowing: D’s ending (close on the feed so the player sees the tip go out as “someone”), and A’s line-up as the look for a story mission rather than for every reward. Add a “Keep it for later” line to the sheet: the reward waits, and a frame sent later doesn’t sit right under your confirmed claim on the feed. Keep the done line honest: “nobody will know it was you” is true of players, but the GM’s file names you, as the tip slip says.
