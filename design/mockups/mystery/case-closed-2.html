F

The interview tapes

The night played back as a police interview: a cassette, a time-stamped transcript, transport keys

Moves · play, skip or scrub the tape; each track is a page. Look · a kraft-label cassette over typed transcript paper.

How F draws the graph

Form
The decided chart (option A, red pen), large: entered on the tape as Exhibit 10, between the verdict and the standings. The deck stays on top, the transcript paper is its frame.
Beats
No beat numbers on the chart; the GM’s lines on the tracks either side carry the story, and the swings say what happened.
Swings
Ringed in red pen and called out by name (“Jonah −30”), with one line each under the chart.
Tap
Scrub the tape: a ruler stops on the chart and the transcript card reads everyone’s score at that second.
Data
pointsLedger (stored today): every line has its time and reason, so each path is replayed from 0 at 19:00. Nothing new is stored.

The ideaThe recap as an interview under caution: the GM’s voice reads the night back, the suspects answer in their own captions, and the tape runs from Bob to the charge.

What’s goodIt has a voice. Templates plus captions make every line real data, and “No comment” is a natural, funny way to show a missing entry. Scrubbing the tape is a natural way to drag the chart’s ruler.

Cost / riskText-heavy: it is a transcript, so photos are small and in the margin. The deck costs 90 px on every page. Scrubbing a tape is a gimmick if it doesn’t also have a plain track list.

G

The redacted dossier

One long classified file: each section’s bars lift as you scroll to it, the verdict only while you hold it

Moves · one continuous scroll; a strip on top counts the sections declassified. Look · white typed paper, red classification bands, black bars.

How G draws the graph

Form
The decided chart (option A, red pen), large, filed as Annex C, Fig. 1: typed caption and key above and below it, the red pen read as the reviewing officer’s.
Beats
Not marked on the figure; the sections before it are the beats, and the swings name them.
Swings
Ringed in red pen and called out (“Jonah −30”); the same three typed as footnotes under the caption.
Tap
Scroll: a black bar over the figure slides right as it comes up the screen, so the night replays left to right. A name lights its line.
Data
pointsLedger (stored today): every line has its time and reason, so each path is replayed from 0 at 19:00. Nothing new is stored.

The ideaThe case as a classified file released to the six of you: the reveal is the black bar lifting, and you control it by scrolling and, for the verdict, by holding.

What’s goodRedaction is a reveal mechanism you can feel, and it handles gaps for free: an empty fact is a bar that never lifts. One scroll suits a phone, and the bar replaying the chart is the best entrance the graph gets.

Cost / riskLifting bars on scroll needs care (reduced motion: no slide, just lifted). A single scroll is long; the clearance strip has to double as a contents jump. White paper is a step away from the manila of A.

H

Bob’s tab

The night as one long till receipt from Head of Steam, clipped into the case folder: every point a priced line

Moves · pull the receipt up; the head lists the twelve items. Look · thermal paper, mono print, red second ink, grey photos.

How H draws the graph

Form
The decided chart (option A, red pen), large, on a slip of chart paper stapled across the receipt, wider than the till roll so it gets the full width.
Beats
Not on the chart: the sections above it are the story, itemised.
Swings
Ringed in red pen and called out on the chart; printed again under it as receipt lines.
Tap
Tap a time: a ruler drops on the chart and the till prints a slip of everyone’s subtotal at that minute.
Data
pointsLedger (stored today): every line has its time and reason, so each path is replayed from 0 at 19:00. Nothing new is stored.

The ideaThe night is a bar tab, so print it as one: the GM’s till itemises every story mission and every point, and the verdict is whoever the bill is charged to.

What’s goodThe ledger is already a list of priced lines, so the receipt shows the real data with no translation, and your page is literally your itemised bill. Funny, cheap to draw (it is mostly text), and it shares well as a long screenshot.

Cost / riskA receipt is monotone: photos go grey and the page is all mono caps, which tires over twelve sections. The chart slip has to break the receipt’s width to be big enough. Money signs on points (“22.00”) may read as real money.

I

The Bob Affair

The night hung as a museum exhibition: a room per page, exhibits under spotlights, plaques, a floor plan to walk it

Moves · walk the floor plan, room to room; the Long Gallery hangs the chart. Look · the dark wall as gallery walls, spotlights, white plaques.

How I draws the graph

Form
The decided chart (option A, red pen), large, hung alone in the Long Gallery in a wood frame and mat, with a museum plaque under it.
Beats
Not on the chart: the rooms before it are the beats, in order.
Swings
Ringed in red pen and called out on the chart; the plaque names them in a sentence.
Tap
Tap a name chip to follow that suspect: their line goes blue biro, the rest fade, and the plaque becomes theirs.
Data
pointsLedger (stored today): every line has its time and reason, so each path is replayed from 0 at 19:00. Nothing new is stored.

The ideaThe night as an exhibition you walk through: each fact is an exhibit with a plaque, the verdict is a single portrait, your file is a postcard from the gift shop.

What’s goodCalm and grown-up; it gives photos room and turns ties and gaps into curatorial language (“attributed jointly”, “in store”, “lent nothing”). Hanging the chart alone in a room gives it the whole screen.

Cost / riskThe most new art: plinths, frames, spotlights, a floor plan. The museum voice can feel slow for a recap read in a pub at midnight.

J

The crawl, retraced

A map-led recap: the route through Didsbury is the spine, and each fact is pinned at the pub where it happened

Moves · swipe the sheet; the map flies to the next place. Look · the case map inked on manila, drop pins, a sheet over it.

How J draws the graph

Form
The decided chart (option A, red pen), large, with the route straightened into a strip above it: each stop as wide as its time on the chart, so the hours read as pubs.
Beats
Not on the chart: the strip above names where the night was at every minute, and the earlier pages pinned each beat to its pub.
Swings
Ringed in red pen and called out on the chart; a “best pub” line under it names each stop’s biggest gainer.
Tap
Tap a stop on the strip: its stretch of the chart is shaded and a card lists what each suspect gained or lost there.
Data
pointsLedger (stored today): every line has its time and reason, so each path is replayed from 0 at 19:00. Nothing new is stored.

The ideaThe night told as the walk: the map you used all night becomes the recap, and every answer sits where it happened. The graph cuts time into pubs.

What’s goodUses what the pack already has (the map module, the stops, their times) and gives the recap a sense of place no other option has. The strip of pubs over the chart makes it easy to talk about: “Fletcher Moss finished Jonah”.

Cost / riskNeeds a place for every fact: most come from the stop the crawl was at, but a photo of somewhere off the route needs its location or the GM. Small text on a map at phone size; the sheet has to carry the reading.

The best two of A–J: J, the crawl retraced, and F, the interview tapes

1J · the crawl, retraced

It is the only direction that belongs to a pub crawl rather than to any murder mystery: the route the six of you walked is the spine, and every answer sits on the map where it happened. It builds on parts the pack already has (the map module, the stops and their times), its pages are a fact each like A, and the strip of pubs over the red-pen chart makes the night easy to talk about afterwards: “Fletcher Moss finished Jonah”.

2F · the interview tapes

It has a voice. Every line is real data (the GM’s lines are templates, the suspects’ lines their own captions), and a missing entry becomes “No comment”, which is funny and needs no explaining. And scrubbing the tape is the most natural way to drag the chart’s ruler: the counter is the clock.

Why not the restA stays the safe, cheapest fallback and the look you already use; D’s solution card is still the one thing worth sharing, and should be the verdict page of whichever wins. H is the funniest and cheapest of round two, but all-caps receipt print tires over twelve pages. G’s redaction is a strong mechanic, but white government paper drifts from the manila. B, C, E and I are the most work for the least reading on a phone.

CombineJ’s map and sheets for the pages, the red-pen chart as page 10 with J’s strip of pubs over it and F’s scrub-to-ruler, D’s solution card on the verdict page, and “No comment” wherever an entry is missing.
