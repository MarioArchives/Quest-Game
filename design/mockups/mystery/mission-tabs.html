A

Index tabs

One manila folder with a numbered index tab per mission along its top edge

Opens on · what needs you most: live and you’re not in → voting and you haven’t voted → the newest. A new mission never pulls you off a tab you chose.

The ideaThe card you know, filed in one folder with an index tab per mission: a typed label with its number and state, and a red dot when it needs you.

What’s goodThe most familiar shape. States read at a glance without opening anything, and closed missions stay one tap away instead of vanishing.

Cost / riskA second row of manila tabs right under Feed / Standings. The white labels and the heading keep them apart, but this is the option most likely to feel like tabs inside tabs. Past five it needs the Earlier fold.

B

Binder tabs

Ring-binder dividers down the folder’s right edge, newest at the top

Opens on · what needs you most: live and you’re not in → voting and you haven’t voted → the newest. A new mission opens itself unless you’re halfway through an entry.

The ideaThe folder gets ring-binder dividers on its right edge, one per mission. The open one joins the page; the rest are kraft, red-edged when they need you.

What’s goodNever competes with Feed / Standings, as it’s on a different edge. Ten tabs fit without scrolling, and your night reads straight down the edge.

Cost / riskTakes about 40px from every card, which squeezes the ballot and the photos. Labels are tiny (a number and a mark), so titles only show once a tab is open.

C

Case log

A strip of chart paper above the file: each mission a numbered rubber stamp, the open one strung to today’s card

Opens on · what needs you most: live and you’re not in → voting and you haven’t voted → the newest. A new mission is thumped onto the log and opens unless you’re mid-entry or mid-vote.

The ideaA case log above the card: the night’s missions as numbered stamps in red (needs you), amber (voting or being checked) or faded ink (filed), the live one ringed by its clock.

What’s goodThe most compact: four with words or ten without fit in one row, no scrolling, nothing folded away. It doubles as your record for the night, looks nothing like the Feed / Standings tabs, and the card below is today’s card.

Cost / riskAt ten the stamps are small (about 30px) and the state words disappear, so it leans on colour and one mark per stamp. The string is decoration that has to be kept in line with the selected stamp.

D

Evidence deck

Today’s cards side by side in a swipeable deck, with a segmented bar for the night above

Opens on · what needs you most: live and you’re not in → voting and you haven’t voted → the newest. The deck slides itself to a new mission unless you’re mid-entry or mid-vote.

The ideaLeave the cards as they are and lay them side by side: swipe between missions, with a segmented bar above showing every mission’s state.

What’s goodEach card keeps its full width. Swiping is a gesture everyone knows, and the bar copes with any number without new UI.

Cost / riskOnly one card shows, so filed results are out of sight unless you go looking. A sideways swipe just under Feed / Standings may be read as switching tab. Cards differ in height, so the page jumps as you swipe.

E

Stepped file

A pile of case files: the open one on top, the rest sticking out underneath as typed edges

Opens on · the top of the pile is what needs you most: live and you’re not in → voting and you haven’t voted → the newest. Under it, newest first.

The ideaA pile of case files instead of a row of tabs: the open mission on top, every other one a labelled edge underneath it.

What’s goodThe edges carry the most: titles, state and your result readable without opening anything. Nothing sits near Feed / Standings.

Cost / riskIt is a stack again, just a much thinner one, and the order shuffles as you open things. Tall cards push the edges out of sight, which needs the extra sticky bar.

Recommendation: C, the case log

It is the only option that shows the whole night (four with words, ten without) in one row with no scrolling and nothing folded away. It doubles as your record (in, missed, won), uses the pack’s own stamps instead of a second set of manila tabs, and keeps today’s card underneath unchanged, so it is the cheapest to build.

Worth borrowing: the slip from A (a new mission never yanks you off a result you are reading) and the typed edges from E for the “Earlier” list if the log ever needs titles.
