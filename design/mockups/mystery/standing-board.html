How a trusted confidant and a person of interest look on the standings

The rules (built today)

Trusted confidant: score above +10. Can tip off the police about another player, once an hour.

Person of interest: score below −10. Gets a police check-in every 30 minutes (a sum to answer within 5 minutes: right +5, wrong or none −5).

Exactly +10 or −10 means neither. The GM moves both lines in Settings.

Same board in every frame

Maya +15, Theo +12: trusted confidants.
Jonah −15, Ellis −22: persons of interest.
In between: You +6 (the lit row), Sam +2, Ruth −3, Priya −9.

Each option replaces today’s small badge beside the name. Everything else is the sheet as built: bars from zero, the face capping the bar, and squares of 5.

Idea
The GM’s two lines are drawn on the chart at ±10 as dashed rules. The paper beyond them is hatched, and each zone is named along the sheet’s edge.
Good
Shows the rule itself and how close everyone is: Priya is one point off and You are four short. It moves when the GM moves the lines, and adds nothing to a row.
Cost
You read standing from where a face sits, not from a word. Hatching adds texture to an already squared sheet.

Good

Cost

Recommendation

A Lines on the chart

It draws the rule instead of labelling it. Everyone can see the line they are heading for and how far off it they are, and when the GM moves a line in Settings, the chart moves with it.

It adds nothing to a row, so the sheet stays as calm as it is today, and it works the same with 6 players or 16.

Worth borrowing: B’s pin, for the places a face appears away from the chart (the feed, a player’s file, the app bar), where there is no line to read against.

Watch for: on a + row, a long name next to the zero line can reach the −10 line. A paper halo keeps it readable.
