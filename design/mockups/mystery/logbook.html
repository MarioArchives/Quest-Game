The Log book: up and down votes, and a top entry kept at the top

The Feed is renamed the Log book, emoji give way to one up or down vote per player per entry, and one entry is kept at the top. Five ways to do it, each with a different control, a different idea of “top” and a different reason to read back through the night.

The same in every option

  1. NameThe Feed tab becomes the Log book: its tab label, a Log book heading on the tab, and the words inside (“Couldn’t load the log book.”, “Nothing logged yet.”, “+ 4 more at Fletcher Moss Pub”).
  2. VotesUp or down, one each per entry. Tap your vote again to take it back; tap the other to switch. The emoji picker goes.
  3. WhoPlayers vote; the GM doesn’t. Nobody votes on their own entry. The game’s and the GM’s notices (new location, mission open, time’s up, standing, announcements) take no votes.
  4. TopOne entry is kept at the top of the Log book. Each option says what makes it top, what a tie does and how it changes hands.

A

Credible or doubtful

Two rubber stamps on every tag; the most credible entry of the night is pinned at the top on an index card. No points

Top · up minus down, all night. Tie · the less doubted wins, then the holder keeps it. Counts · both, on the stamps.

The ideaMake a vote say something in the story: is this entry credible? The stamps are the case file’s own ink, and the most credible thing anyone logged tonight is pinned up on the wall.

What’s goodThe words carry the game: you stamp Maya’s photo credible because it incriminates Sam. Both counts are honest (4 to 1 says more than +3). “To stamp” counts give a plain, finite job at every stop.

Cost / risk“Credible” fits evidence better than a confirmed claim or a photo of a pint; the words may feel forced there. Two labelled buttons are wider than icons. A night-long top can stall on one early entry.

Data changeThe shared votes, plus game/state.top {feedId, score, since}, kept by the vote trigger in a transaction: a post takes it only by beating the holder’s score, or matching it with fewer downs. The previous holder goes into top.previous for the card underneath.

B

Stop press

Arrows in the margin and only the net score; the top is a newsprint headline from the last hour’s votes. No points

Top · up minus down, counting votes cast in the last hour. Tie · the one voted on last. Counts · the net only.

The ideaMake the top news: the headline is whatever the group is voting up right now, from any stop. Old entries can make the front page again if people dig them up, which is the reason to read back.

What’s goodThe top keeps moving all night, so it’s worth checking. The arrows are small and sit in the margin, out of the text’s way. One number per tag keeps it calm.

Cost / riskNet only hides a fight: +1 might be 4 up and 3 down. Ties and the hour are harder to explain than “most votes”. A quiet hour leaves an old headline up (it keeps “since 22:44”). Needs the timer, not just the trigger.

Data changeThe shared votes (each keeps its createdAt), plus the headline worked out after each vote and every 5 minutes by runTick: a collection-group query on reactions from the last hour, summed per post, kept as game/state.headline {feedId, score, since, previous}. A new collection-group index; the flame is the same sum per stop.

C

Pick of the stop

A pencil tick and cross with tally marks; every stop keeps its own top entry clipped to its folder. No points

Top · up minus down, at each stop. Tie · shared: both clipped on. Counts · both, as tallies.

The ideaEvery stop gets a best-of. The night becomes five or six picks, one per pub, so a filed stop has something on its front worth opening, and the stop you’re at has a pick still up for grabs.

What’s goodSomething to win at every stop, not one entry that runs away with the night. Tallies show both sides without a minus sign. Picks need no new state: the app works them out from the posts it already has.

Cost / riskNot one top for the whole night (the brief’s single top entry becomes one per stop). A new stop starts with no pick. Tally marks get long past about 8 votes. “Read” is per phone, so it doesn’t follow you to another device.

Data changeOnly the shared votes. The picks come from the posts the app loads (grouped by stop as the Log book already does), so no new server state; ties are shown, not broken. “Read” is kept on the phone (a last-seen time per stop in local storage), not in Firestore.

D

Exhibit at each stop

Thumbprints to admit or throw out; each stop’s best entry is bagged as evidence and pays its poster +2 when the group leaves. Changes scoring

Top · up minus down at this stop, 3 admits at least. Tie · the +2 is split. Counts · inked prints, both.

The ideaGive votes weight: the group decides one exhibit per stop and it scores. You read back because the clock is running and your print can move two points.

What’s goodThe strongest pull to look and vote. Points make the top matter, and the stop’s close is a natural moment (“admitted”) to celebrate.

Cost / riskChanges scoring. Votes become tactics: throw out a rival’s entry before the clock, trade admits. Your honest vote can cost you (Ruth’s own roll call was second). Anonymous tips are left out. More to explain.

Data changeThe shared votes; then a new scoring event, exhibit, in every rule set (+2, or +1 each on a tie, a pack param), its ledger reason and a House rules row. runTick settles a stop when the next one starts, through applyScore, and seals stops/{id}.exhibit {feedId, uid, points}. Cases in scoring.test.ts, both stores.

E

Top of the log

Thumbs up and down, ups only on show; the night’s most liked entry hangs first, gold-edged; a pile to swipe through what you missed. No points

Top · most ups, all night. Tie · the holder keeps it. Counts · ups only; 3 downs fold an entry as disputed.

The ideaKeep it as quick as liking a post. Ups make the top; downs only act together, folding an entry three people doubt. Reading back is a pile you can clear in a minute.

What’s goodNothing to learn. The pile turns reading back into a short game and gets the most votes in. No negative numbers on anyone’s entry, and the folded “disputed” lines keep junk out of the way.

Cost / riskLeast in the case-file world: thumbs are any app. Most ups favours early entries and photos. Hidden downs can surprise someone when their entry folds. The award needs a new award rule in the engine.

Data changeThe shared votes. The top is a query the app can run itself (orderBy up desc, createdAt asc, which needs an index), and “disputed” is down ≥ 3, so no new server state. The pile reads your votes from the votes map. The award: a new criterion in endGame and the pack’s award copy.

Recommendation: A, credible or doubtful, with C’s picks on the filed stops

A does what you asked most directly: one entry kept at the top all night, and a vote that means something in this game. Stamping Maya’s photo credible is a move in the mystery, not just a like, and the rubber stamps are the case file’s own ink. Both counts show, so 4 to 1 reads as a real argument. Its “to stamp” counts give each stop a short, finishable job, which is the plainest push to read back. No points, so scoring stays as it is.

Borrow two things: C’s pick clipped to each filed stop’s folder (no extra data, and a reason to open old stops), and E’s catch-up pile behind the “Next to stamp” pill, for stamping quickly. Leave out B’s rolling hour (hard to explain) and D’s points (votes turn tactical).

Rules: only ±1, not on your own entry or a notice. Lancaster keeps emoji behind a pack switch.
